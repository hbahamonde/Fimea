Public funding of high-cost cancer medicines requires choices between patient benefit and limited resources. Public preferences may depend on the information and justifications provided. This article examines how rescue-oriented and resource-preservation justifications affect public funding preferences in Finland. Drawing on prospect theory, we expect rescue and resource-preservation justifications to shift funding preferences in different directions. We examine these expectations in a survey experiment embedded in the 2021 Finnish Medicines Barometer. Respondents evaluated an identical clinical vignette and were randomly assigned to rescue or resource-preservation justifications, or to a control without supplementary information. Relative to the common-vignette control, rescue information reduces rejection, whereas resource-preservation information reduces unconditional funding. Both treatments also increase “I don’t know” responses.
